Shuo Chen

MPower Professor of Biostatistics and Bioinformatics
University of Maryland School of Medicine

I develop statistical and machine-learning methods for complex, dependent biomedical data. My work focuses on reliable individual-level inference, cross-study reproducibility, and trustworthy AI/ML.

My research spans network statistics, high-throughput features, and spatiotemporal structure in neuroimaging and multi-omics data, with applications in real-world evidence, clinical trials, genetics, cancer, and population health.

I direct Biostatistics and Data Science at the Institute for Health Computing and the Maryland Psychiatric Research Center, and serve as Graduate Director of the PhD program in Biostatistics and Medical Data Science.

research

  • Learning graph structures of inter-correlated imaging and omics data and their inference

    Develop network and graph extraction methods to learn latent correlation patterns in high-dimensional imaging and omics data, improving the accuracy, reproducibility, and replicability of data analysis.

  • Machine learning/AI regression bias correction

    Like ordinary least squares models, machine learning and AI regression models are affected by the “regression to the mean” issue, which introduces systematic bias and poses a major challenge for inference with predicted outcomes (biased estimates).

  • Clinical trial design and analysis

    Experience with the design and analysis of more than 40 trials evaluating drug efficacy, bioequivalence, and diagnostic devices, including Bayesian computation of probability of success.

  • New statistical procedures

    Develop mediation models with multiple mediators and outcomes, missing-data imputation methods for high-dimensional data, ML/AI-based causal inference, mixed models for repeated measures with high-dimensional data, and factor analysis for high-dimensional data with complex dependence patterns.

Areas: machine learning, clinical trials, network statistics, multi-omics, neuroimaging, Bayesian modeling, and spatiotemporal models.
